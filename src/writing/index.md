We write plain English, kept minimal. Our readers are busy and usually
scanning, so every line has to make sense the first time they read it.

This page is the short version. The full rules, with examples for every kind of
writing from button labels to legal pages, live in the
studio’s writing skill.
When this page and the skill disagree, the skill is right.

## The style

Point first
: The first sentence carries what the reader needs most, and the detail follows it.

Whole sentences of a natural length
: Keep the small words that carry logic, like "because" and "so". Avoid both clipped fragments and long chains of clauses.

Say it once
: Cut words that repeat, hedge or warm up, and stop when the point is made.

Everyday words
: Use the word you'd say out loud, and name the actual thing rather than its category.

Easy to scan
: Keep one idea to a paragraph, put the key word early in the line, and use a list for steps.

Same word, same thing
: Pick one term for each thing and use it on every screen, email and page.

Sound like a person
: Write the way one person explains something to another, never like a system, a brand or a mascot.

## One message, three ways

Too short
: “Order delayed. Supplier issue. We’ll email.”

Too long
: “Unfortunately, it looks like your order has been delayed, as our supplier wasn’t able to meet the date they originally gave us, but rest assured we’ll be in touch as soon as we hear anything.”

Right
: “Your order is running late because our supplier missed their date. We’ll email you as soon as it ships.”

The short one reads like a telegram, and the long one buries the news. The
right one uses two whole sentences, carries the reason with “because” and says
nothing twice.

## Mechanics

Canadian spelling, contractions, figures for numbers and sentence case. No em
dashes, no exclamation marks and no emoji.
